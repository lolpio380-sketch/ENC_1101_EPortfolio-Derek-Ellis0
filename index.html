WHO IS ACTUALLY BEHIND THIS?
NOT JUST
A GRADE.

I'm Derek Ellis, and this portfolio documents my experience in ENC 1101.


Before this class, I mostly thought of writing as something you finished and turned in. During this semester, I started looking at it more like a process. You make something, look at it again, find what is wrong, and change it.


That idea actually connects with things I already like: making music, playing chess, and messing around with ideas until they finally work.

"I lost my first real chess game in four moves."

My personal essay starts with a chess game in Mr. Moore's elementary school classroom. I walked into the game already assuming that being academically gifted meant I would probably be good at chess too.

I was wrong.

The game ended almost immediately. But the interesting part wasn't losing. It was realizing that knowing something academically doesn't automatically mean you know how to do it.

03 / PROCESS
THE DRAFT IS NOT THE FINISHED PRODUCT
IDEA →
DRAFT →
CHANGE.
01

IDEA

Start with something worth talking about. For my personal essay, that was a chess game that ended way faster than I expected.

02

DRAFT

Get the story down first. It doesn't have to be perfect. The important part is having something to work with.

03

FEEDBACK

Look at the writing from another perspective. Repeated phrases, unclear sections, and organization become easier to notice.

04

REVISION

Keep what works. Cut what doesn't. Make the writing sound more natural and make the point clearer.

06 / REFLECTION
FINAL THOUGHTS / ENC 1101

I started this course thinking mostly about finishing assignments.


I am leaving it thinking more about how I make them.


Writing is not really that different from making a beat or playing a game of chess. You start with an idea, make a move, see what happens, and sometimes realize you need to go back and change something.


The first version doesn't have to be perfect. It just has to give you something to work with.
